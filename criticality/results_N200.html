Method notes

  1. Avalanche definition (identical in both pipelines): an avalanche is a run of consecutive time steps with population activity strictly above θ; duration T = number of steps, size S = Σ(activity − θ) over those steps. Avalanches are detected per run, then the size/duration events are pooled across the 50 runs into one fit — raw traces are never concatenated, so no artificial avalanche bridges two simulations.
  2. Exponents are discrete power-law MLE fits (tumbleweed’s tplfit estimator). “Automatic” lets the pipeline pick xmin/xmax by a Kolmogorov–Smirnov search; “manual” fixes them to the windows hand-chosen in the paper. Note the automatic size window under the half-mean threshold (10–1558) independently lands on nearly the paper’s choice (10–1500).
  3. DCC (deviation from crackling): |(β−1)/(α−1) − measured slope of log ⟨S⟩(T)| over the duration window; smaller is closer to the scaling relation.
  4. Agreement: with threshold and windows matched, the two toolchains (tumbleweed MLE vs the powerlaw package) agree to 4–5 decimal places on both exponents — they measure the same thing; the spread across the grid above is entirely the threshold and window choices.

Reproducibility: all pooled fits above are byte-identical across three independent executions of the pipeline. Per-run branching ratio on this data: 0.881 ± 0.083 (mrestimator, exponential fit), threshold-independent.
